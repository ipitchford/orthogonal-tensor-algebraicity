## Recommendation: Major revisions for the requested novelty-led publication

The revision makes a genuine mathematical enlargement: every fixed nonzero algebraic O(m)-representation, rather than only invariants, has an algebraic tensor-multiplicity series exactly in dimensions at most three. Its explicit low-rank formulas and bounded-output contraction argument are useful additions. I found no immediate mathematical counterexample in the extension. However, the stated user objective is to establish a genuinely new, nontrivial classification with stronger significance than the earlier high-two-star assessment. The current evidence does not establish that objective. I do not endorse high-three-star originality/significance or irreversible publication presented as having cleared that bar.

This recommendation is not a finding that the theorem is old, false, or unpublishable in every venue. It is an unresolved, unusually direct prior-art gate. The manuscript's own statement that historical novelty remains bounded uncertainty is accurate and should be preserved.

## What the extension really adds

1. The parity argument handles the full disconnected orthogonal group, including both central eigenspaces and nonirreducible genuine representations. Positivity avoids a hidden cancellation claim about virtual representations.
2. Character weighting multiplies the scalar leading constant by `(dim W) m^epsilon`; it leaves the logarithmic/transcendental-amplitude obstruction intact. This yields the all-fixed-type conclusion, not just finitely checked examples.
3. The low-rank formulas specify every irreducible type, not merely existence of some algebraic representation. The D-finiteness proof supplies the needed module finite-generation hypothesis.

These additions provide a coherent classification and a useful consequence: selecting a fixed covariant type cannot restore algebraicity above dimension three. Nevertheless the weighted Laplace step is short once the scalar theorem is available, and the dimension-ratio principle is classical. Counting the enlarged quantifier as a new research method, or treating D-finiteness as new, would overstate the contribution.

## Primary-source checks and limits

- Dicks's author-hosted erratum was inspected directly: <https://mat.uab.cat/~dicks/ADFerr.html>. It corrects a coefficient on page 207 from 93 to 91 at degree eight. It does **not** identify the group, representation, or complete series. The matching coefficient is an excellent retrieval lead but not an established O(3) collision. In particular, SO(3) and O(3) invariant series agree in even degrees but differ in odd degrees; this fingerprint alone cannot distinguish them.
- Tate--Zelditch's author-hosted paper was inspected at its abstract, Theorem 8/Biane comparison, and adjacent discussion: <https://sites.math.northwestern.edu/~zelditch/Preprints/article.pdf>, DOI <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfa.2004.01.004>. It explicitly concerns connected compact groups and dimension-proportional irreducible multiplicity asymptotics. This supports the prior-work table's claim that the limit-ratio principle is old, without by itself resolving the disconnected-group classification here.
- Grabiner--Magyar, Theorem 2/Section 3.3, identifies tensor multiplicities with chamber walks and the corresponding exponential generating functions. The normalization caveat in the comparison is essential: exponential and ordinary algebraicity do not transfer automatically. Author copy: <https://users.math.msu.edu/users/magyarp/papers/RandomWalk.pdf>.
- Bounded targeted searches did not produce a decisive primary-source statement of the exact all-fixed-O(m)-type algebraicity classification. This negative search result is not evidence sufficient to establish novelty. I did not obtain full texts of Almkvist--Dicks--Formanek 1985 or Cohen--Regev 1988, and therefore cannot clear those leads.

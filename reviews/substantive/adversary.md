## Strongest counterargument

The strongest case against the requested publication is not that the theorem is false. It is that an elegant, apparently correct rederivation plus a natural character-weighted extension has not yet been distinguished from existing tensor-invariant theory closely enough to support the requested genuinely new high-quality classification. The revised comparison itself says precisely this. Its explicit recognition that the scalar series is the Hilbert series of the invariant tensor algebra is decisive: the relevant literature is not merely about an analogous commutative invariant ring with a different grading. Almkvist–Dicks–Formanek's 1985 paper addresses this directly matching setting, yet its full text remains uninspected. The accessible erratum really does correct a coefficient to 91 in degree eight, agreeing with the rank-three fingerprint; that is a strong relevance signal, not proof that the present classification was already known.

The extension to all fixed nonzero representations improves the mathematical scope. Nevertheless its high-rank argument simply inserts a character whose values at the two dominant points are prescribed by dimension and central parity. The limiting dimension ratio is already acknowledged as classical, and the finite-generation argument supports application of an existing D-finiteness theorem. It therefore does not automatically transform an uncertain scalar priority claim into a major new contribution. A publisher could value the exposition, normalization audit, and reproducible diagnostics as a synthesis, but that would be a different editorial proposition from the user's requested demonstrated new classification. Honest qualification is necessary; it is not itself the missing evidence of historical novelty.

The mathematical proof is unusually explicit about parity, normalization, and the limits of finite checks. These are genuine strengths.

## Stress tests of the mathematical claim

- Central parity: compatible powers give the same sign at both trace maxima, even when both maxima lie in SO(m). No cancellation defect found.
- Reducible W: splitting into the two central-parity summands and using algebraic parity extraction prevents cancellation between the two series. The exclusion of virtual differences and W=0 is essential and present.
- Character amplitude: multiplying the scalar coefficient by the positive integer `dim(W) m^epsilon` preserves the transcendental square-root amplitude. A possibly non-real character away from the identity does not invalidate dominated convergence; its norm is bounded on the compact group.
- Low ranks: O(2) reflection mass and the factor two from the rotation character are handled consistently. The O(3) spin recurrence has the correct exceptional boundary at spin zero, and its parity extraction treats both extensions of an SO(3) irreducible.
- Finite generation: self-duality, polynomial matrix coefficients of O(m), complete reducibility, and pairing contractions support the asserted bound. Bounded Schur length then supplies the stated D-finiteness input. I found no illicit deduction that an arbitrary submodule of a finitely generated tca module must be finitely generated.
- Scalar radial obstruction: the algebraic coefficient field is justified by rational-coefficient descent; the asymptotic argument is radial and does not improperly assume a unique dominant complex singularity.

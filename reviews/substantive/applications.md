## Scope and assessment

The fixed-covariant theorem has a meaningful mathematical consequence beyond the original yes/no counterexample. For every fixed nonzero genuine algebraic O(m) representation W, its ordinary tensor-multiplicity series has the same dimension-three algebraicity boundary. In dimensions at least four, replacing the scalar invariant by a preferred fixed symmetry type cannot evade the obstruction. The explicit O(2) and O(3) formulas make the positive side useful and concrete. The contraction argument also supplies the needed finite-generation bridge to the existing D-finiteness theorem rather than assuming it.

The important qualifications are correctly present: W is fixed, finite-dimensional and genuine rather than virtual; the series counts multiplicities, not general isotypic dimensions; the grading is tensor degree, not a finite-variable commutative invariant-ring grading. These boundaries prevent several misleading cross-disciplinary interpretations.

## Priority findings

### P1 — Historical novelty remains a publication blocker for this request

The comparison explicitly reports that Almkvist–Dicks–Formanek studies the identical tensor-invariant object, with a rank-three coefficient fingerprint, but its main text has not been inspected. Cohen–Regev is another direct asymptotic lead, and Handelman's representation-ring limit-ratio work remains uninspected. Consequently the bundle itself says it does not establish the user's requested genuinely new contribution. I agree with that conclusion. A broad quantifier over W cannot resolve this evidential gap, especially when the weighted character asymptotic and dimension-ratio phenomenon already have substantial antecedents.

Required resolution: obtain a sufficiently authoritative statement of the directly relevant prior theorems and compare the ordinary/exponential normalization, group, allowed representations, and algebraicity conclusion explicitly. If their results subsume this classification, identify a genuinely additional theorem or decline publication under the requested significance objective. Do not silently replace that objective with publication as an expository candidate, and do not label the current historical-newness claim cleared merely because all proofs or replay checks pass.

### P2 — Keep the computational consequence structural, not algorithmic

Section 5 correctly excludes algebraic equations and polynomial systems whose relevant component is independently known to be algebraic. It does not prove that coefficient computation is hard, that differential equations have large order, or that no efficient recurrence exists. Preserve these qualifications in the public abstract, audio and thumbnail. A suitable accurate summary is: “Every fixed covariant remains D-finite, but none is algebraic in dimension four or higher.” Avoid “no finite formula,” “no polynomial method,” or algorithmic impossibility language.

### P2 — Integrate the strengthened theorem into the main narrative

If the priority gate clears, the full fixed-covariant theorem should appear in the main manuscript's abstract/introduction and conclusion, with its proof either integrated or clearly designated as a companion theorem. The scalar manuscript presently concludes only the scalar classification; the addition carries much of the proposed revised significance. A publication reader should not need to infer from packaging which theorem is the principal contribution.

### P3 — Add one worked nontrivial type as an explanatory aid

The determinant character, contrasted between O(3) and O(4), would make the extension tangible without claiming a new application. In O(3), use the j=0, odd-central-parity extraction; in O(4), the general theorem excludes algebraicity although D-finiteness persists. This illustrates a real use of the theorem and avoids presenting the already familiar dimension-ratio limit as a new consequence of independent significance.

## Defensible rating

**Overall: high two-star at this evidence stage.** The extension is mathematically substantive relative to the reviewed scalar manuscript and the consequences are more precise. I would regard low three-star as plausible if the close prior-art comparisons establish a genuinely unreported classification. I cannot defend high three-star now: no new application, effective differential-equation bound, or independent algorithmic advance is established, and the historical priority of the central classification remains open. This is a significance judgement, not a finding that the theorem is false; economy of proof is also not itself a defect.

Publication recommendation: retain the proof package and repairs, but keep the requested research-publication significance/novelty gate open until P1 is resolved.
